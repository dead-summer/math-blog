## Abstract

In terms of stress and displacement, the linear elasticity problem is discretized by a least-squares finite element method. In the case of a convex polygonal domain, the stress is approximated by the lowest-order Raviart-Thomas-Nédélec flux element,and the displacement by the linear \( {C}^{0} \) element. We obtain coerciveness and optimal \( {H}^{1} \) , \( {L}^{2} \) and \( H \) (div)-error bounds,uniform in Lamé constant \( \lambda \) ,for displacement and stress,respectively. Our method also allows the use of any other combination of conforming elements for stress and displacement,e.g., \( {C}^{0} \) elements for all variables.

## 1. Introduction

As is well known,lower-order displacement-based \( {C}^{0} \) finite elements of order not greater than 3 for the linear elasticity problem usually suffer from a volumetric locking phenomenon as the elastic material becomes incompressible,i.e.,as Lamé constant \( \lambda \) goes to infinity,cf. [21]. To overcome this locking phenomenon, many finite element methods have been proposed, e.g., see [1-8,10-12,20,24-26].

Among others, it is of physical interest to approximate stress and displacement simultaneously. This can be usually done from a mixed finite element method based on the Hellinger-Reissner variational principle. However, for this two-field formulation it is a non-trivial task to select a pair of approximating subspaces for stress and displacement such that \( Z \) -ellipticity and Inf-Sup condition are fufilled, uniformly in the mesh-size of the triangulation, cf. [2,11,5,10,13]. Especially, although the popular Ravi-art-Thomas-Nédélec (RTN) flux elements of lowest-order is a desirable candidate for interpolating stress, it cannot be employed straightforwardly. Moreover, to compute the resulting discrete approximation one must solve a saddle-point system, which is sometimes a source of troublesome (its eigen-values are both positive and negative and the traditional iterative methods such as conjugate gradient are not convergent).

For these reasons, when designing a mixed discretization for stress-displacement of linear elasticity, the following mathematical considerations are very important: (1) Z-ellipticity and Inf-Sup condition can be circumvented; (2) the resulting linear system is symmetric, positive definite; (3) conforming elements of lowest-order can be used for stress-displacement (e.g., the lowest-order RTN element for stress and the linear \( {C}^{0} \) element for displacement) and (4) \( {H}^{1},{L}^{2} \) and \( H \) (div)-error bounds for displacement and stress are optimal both in order of approximation as well as required regularity and are uniform in \( \lambda \) .

During the last decade, mixed methods of least-squares type have been receiving increasing attention in both the engineering and mathematics communities, see [14] for a survey and see [15] for many applications. In this paper, we develop and analyze a first-order system least-squares mixed finite element method for the linear elasticity problem in two and three dimensions, where stress and displacement appear as simultaneous variables. Our method can accommodate all these considerations as above.

Specifically, we first introduce a first-order system, consisting of the stress-strain relation, the equilibrium condition, the volumetric constitutive equation and the symmetry equation of the stress. Based on \( {L}^{2} \) -norm,we define a least-squares minimization problem for the residuals associated with the first-order system. Trivially, a weak problem is derived. In the case of a convex polygonal domain, triangulated into triangles or quadrilaterals (or tetrahedrons or rectangular parallelepiped elements in three dimensions), the (nonsymmetric) stress is approximated by the lowest-order RTN flux element, while the displacement is approximated by the linear \( {C}^{0} \) element. Our analysis shows that this new method verifies (1)-(4) labelled as above. Of course, a combination of higher-order elements may be also employed.

We remark that the stress may be also symmetric. Consequently, both stress and displacement can be approximated by any \( {C}^{0} \) elements,e.g.,the linear \( {C}^{0} \) element for both stress and displacement. In this case, the error bounds are sub-optimal, but from an engineering point of view it is preferable using regular finite element spaces of piecewise polynomials with equal-order interpolation for both the trial and test functions.

If using unequal-order \( {C}^{0} \) approximations for (symmetric) stress and displacement (e.g.,quadratic \( {C}^{0} \) element for displacement and linear \( {C}^{0} \) element for stress),our method still results in a sub-optimal convergence. However, for this case we can adopt a remedy strategy to ensure that the error bounds can be optimal both in order of approximation as well as required regularity. That is to say, some terms are weighted by a factor \( {h}^{-2} \) in the least-squares functional. Thus,from the coerciveness we first obtain a sub-optimal error bound and we then recover the optimal-order of the error bound with the help of the well-known Aubin-Nitsche duality argument with a perturbation trick [19].

The outline of this paper is as follows: Section 2 includes the equation of linear elasticity and notations. In Section 3, a least-squares scheme is formulated and coerciveness is established. In Section 4, we obtain the \( {H}^{1} \) and \( H \) (div)-error bounds for displacement and stress,respectively. In Section 5,we obtain the \( {L}^{2} \) - error bound for the displacement. Section 6 concerns the unequal-order \( {C}^{0} \) approximations for stress and displacement. The last section summarizes all the conclusions.

## 2. Preliminaries

#### 2.1.The linear elasticity

Let \( \Omega  \subset  {\Re }^{d}\left( {d = 2,3}\right) \) be a bounded and connected domain with a Lipschitz continuous boundary \( \Gamma  = {\overline{\Gamma }}_{D} \cup  {\overline{\Gamma }}_{N}\left( {{\Gamma }_{D} \cap  {\Gamma }_{N} = \phi }\right) \) . Given a volume force \( \mathbf{f} : \Omega  \rightarrow  {\mathfrak{R}}^{d} \) ,the linear elasticity problem is to find the displacement \( \mathbf{u} : \Omega  \rightarrow  {\Re }^{d} \) and the stress tensor \( \mathbf{\sigma } : \Omega  \rightarrow  {\Re }_{\text{ sym }}^{d \times  d} \mathrel{\text{ := }} \left\{  {\mathbf{\tau } \in  {\Re }^{d \times  d};\mathbf{\tau } = {\mathbf{\tau }}^{\mathrm{T}}}\right\} \) such that (cf. [18])

\[\mathbf{\sigma } = {2\mu }\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{u}\right)  + \lambda \operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}\operatorname{Id},\;\text{ (stress-strain relation), }\]

\[\operatorname{div}\mathbf{\sigma } =  - \mathbf{f},\;\text{ (equilibrium condition), } \tag{2.1}\]

\[{\left. \mathbf{u}\right| }_{{\Gamma }_{D}} = \mathbf{0},{\left. \;\mathbf{\sigma }\mathbf{n}\right| }_{{\Gamma }_{N}} = \mathbf{0},\;\text{ (boundary condition), }\]

where \( \left( {\mu ,\lambda }\right)  \in  \left( {{\mu }_{1},{\mu }_{2}}\right)  \times  \left( {0,\infty }\right) \) are the Lamé constants where \( 0 < {\mu }_{1} < {\mu }_{2}\left( {\lambda  = \infty }\right. \) corresponds to an incompressible material),Id is the \( d \times  d \) identity matrix and \( \mathbf{n} \) is the exterior unit normal vector to \( \Gamma \) ,and \( \varepsilon \left( \mathbf{u}\right) \) is the strain tensor given by \( \mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{u}\right)  = \frac{1}{2}\left( {\nabla \mathbf{u} + {\left( \nabla \mathbf{u}\right) }^{\mathrm{T}}}\right) \) ,where \( \nabla \) is the gradient operator and \( {\left( \nabla \mathbf{u}\right) }^{\mathrm{T}} \) is the transposition of \( \nabla \mathbf{u} \) . And,div \( \mathbf{u} = \mathop{\sum }\limits_{{j = 1}}^{d}\widehat{\partial }{u}_{j}/\partial {x}_{j} \) and \( \operatorname{div}\mathbf{\sigma } = {\left( \mathop{\sum }\limits_{{j = 1}}^{d}\partial {\sigma }_{1j}/\partial {x}_{j},\ldots ,\mathop{\sum }\limits_{{j = 1}}^{d}\partial {\sigma }_{dj}/\partial {x}_{j}\right) }^{\mathrm{T}} \) .

Remark 2.1. The volumetric constitutive equation is as follows:

\[\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma }\right) ,\;\left( {\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma }\right)  \mathrel{\text{ := }} \mathop{\sum }\limits_{{i = 1}}^{d}{\sigma }_{ii}}\right) , \tag{2.2}\]

which may be easily derived from the stress-strain relation in (2.1). Clearly, the symmetry of the stress means that

\[\sigma  = {\sigma }^{\mathrm{T}}. \tag{2.3}\]

### 2.2. Notations

Let \( {H}^{k}\left( \Omega \right) \) denote the Sobolev space of order \( k \in  \Re \) ,and \( \parallel  \cdot  {\parallel }_{k} \) denote the corresponding norm,cf. [9,17]. Let \( \left( {\cdot , \cdot  }\right) \) denote the inner product in \( {L}^{2}\left( \Omega \right)  \equiv  {H}^{0}\left( \Omega \right) \) . These notations \( \parallel  \cdot  {\parallel }_{k} \) and \( \left( {\cdot , \cdot  }\right) \) will be also used for \( {\left( {H}^{k}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \) and \( {\left( {H}^{k}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d \times  d} \) .

We introduce \( {H}_{{\Gamma }_{D}}^{1}\left( \Omega \right)  = \left\{  {v \in  {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) ;{\left. v\right| }_{{\Gamma }_{D}} = 0}\right\} \) ,with norm \( \parallel  \cdot  {\parallel }_{1} \) ,and \( H\left( {\operatorname{div};\Omega }\right)  = \left\{  {\mathbf{q} \in  {\left( {L}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d}}\right. \) ; \( \operatorname{div}\mathbf{q} \in  {L}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \} \) ,with norm \( \parallel  \cdot  {\parallel }_{0,\operatorname{div}} = \sqrt{\parallel  \cdot  {\parallel }_{0}^{2} + \parallel \operatorname{div} \cdot  {\parallel }_{0}^{2}} \) .

If \( {\Gamma }_{D} \neq  \phi \) ,we define

\[U = {\left( {H}_{{\Gamma }_{D}}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d}, \tag{2.4}\]

else, we define

\[U = {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \cap  {\mathbf{Z}}^{c} = \left\{  {\mathbf{v} \in  {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d};{\int }_{\Omega }\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0},\;{\int }_{\Omega }\nabla  \times  \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}}\right\}  , \tag{2.5}\]

where \( {\mathbf{Z}}^{c} \) denotes the complement in \( {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \) of \( \mathbf{Z} \) ,the space of infinitesimal rigid motions defined by (cf. [1,18,9])

\[\mathbf{Z} = \left\{  {\mathbf{v} \in  {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d};\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right)  = \mathbf{0}}\right\}   = {\left( {\mathcal{P}}_{0}\right) }^{d} + {\left( {\mathcal{P}}_{0}\right) }^{d} \times  \mathbf{x}, \tag{2.6}\]

where \( {\mathcal{P}}_{0} \) is the space of constants. Note that in the case \( {\Gamma }_{D} = \varnothing \) the source function \( \mathbf{f} \) is required to be \( {L}^{2} \) - orthogonal to \( \mathbf{Z} \) .

If \( {\Gamma }_{N} \neq  \varnothing \) ,we define

\[{\sum }_{0} = \left\{  {\mathbf{\tau } \in  {\left( H\left( \operatorname{div};\Omega \right) \right) }^{d};{\left. \mathbf{\tau }\mathbf{n}\right| }_{{\Gamma }_{N}} = \mathbf{0}}\right\}  , \tag{2.7}\]

else, we define

\[{\sum }_{0} = \left\{  {\mathbf{\tau } \in  {\left( H\left( \operatorname{div};\mathbf{\Omega }\right) \right) }^{d};{\int }_{\Omega }\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\tau }\right)  = 0}\right\}  , \tag{2.8}\]

where

\[\operatorname{tr}\left( \tau \right)  = \mathop{\sum }\limits_{{i = 1}}^{d}{\tau }_{ii} \tag{2.9}\]

is the trace of \( \tau \) .

In the case \( \tau \) being symmetric,we define

\[{\sum }_{0}^{S} = {\sum }_{0} \cap  {\Re }_{\text{ sym }}^{d \times  d} \tag{2.10}\]

If \( {\Gamma }_{N} = \varnothing \) ,we define

\[Q = {L}_{0}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \tag{2.11}\]

else, we define

\[Q = {L}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) . \tag{2.12}\]

The norms for \( U,{\sum }_{0} \) (or \( {\sum }_{0}^{S} \) ) and \( Q \) are denoted by \( \parallel  \cdot  {\parallel }_{1},\parallel  \cdot  {\parallel }_{0,\text{ div }} \) and \( \parallel  \cdot  {\parallel }_{0} \) ,respectively. We will use \( U \) for the displacement,and \( {\sum }_{0} \) (or \( {\sum }_{0}^{S} \) ) for the nonsymmetric (or symmetric) stress. \( Q \) will be only used in the establishment of the \( {L}^{2} \) -error bound.

The letter \( C \) (with or without subscripts) stands for a generic positive constant which may take different values at different occurrences,but it is always independent of the Lamé constant \( \lambda \) and the mesh-size \( h \) in the triangulation of \( \Omega \) .

### 3.A least-squares mixed formulation

In this section, we formulate a least-squares mixed problem and obtain the coerciveness.

A first-order system for (2.1)-(2.3) is as follows:

\[\left\{  \begin{array}{l} \frac{{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} + {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{{D}^{\mathrm{T}}}}{2} = {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{u}\right) , \\  \operatorname{div}\mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma }\right) , \\  \mathbf{\sigma } = {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{\mathrm{T}}, \\  \operatorname{div}\mathbf{\sigma } =  - f, \\  {u}_{{u}_{{t}_{0}}} = 0,\;\sigma {u}_{{t}_{0{t}_{0}}} = 0, \end{array}\right. \tag{3.1}\]

where

\[{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} = \mathbf{\sigma } - \frac{1}{d}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma }\right) \operatorname{Id}\]

is the deviatoric tensor,and \( {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{u}\right)  = \mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{u}\right)  - \frac{1}{d}\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}\operatorname{Id} \) .

Define a \( {L}^{2} \) functional for (3.1) with nonsymmetric stress as follows:

\[\mathcal{J}\left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma };\mathbf{f}}\right)  = {\begin{Vmatrix}\frac{{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} + {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{{D}^{\mathrm{T}}}}{2} - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{u}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}^{2} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\left( \mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{\mathrm{T}}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}^{2} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u} - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma }\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}^{2} + \parallel \mathbf{{div}}\mathbf{\sigma } + \mathbf{f}{\parallel }_{0}^{2}. \tag{3.2}\]

The minimization problem for (3.2) is to find \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0} \) such that

\[\mathcal{J}\left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma };\mathbf{f}}\right)  = \mathop{\inf }\limits_{{\left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0}}}\mathcal{J}\left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau };\mathbf{f}}\right) , \tag{3.3}\]

the corresponding weak problem of which is to find \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0} \) such that

\[\mathcal{L}\left( {\left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) ;\left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau }}\right) }\right)  \mathrel{\text{ := }} \left( {\frac{{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} + {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{{D}^{\mathrm{T}}}}{2} - {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{u}\right) ,\frac{{\mathbf{\tau }}^{D} + {\mathbf{\tau }}^{{D}^{\mathrm{T}}}}{2} - {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)\]

\[+ \left( {\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u} - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma }\right) ,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v} - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\tau }\right) }\right)  + \left( {\operatorname{div}\mathbf{\sigma },\operatorname{div}\mathbf{\tau }}\right)\]

\[+ \frac{1}{4}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{\mathrm{T}},\mathbf{\tau } - {\mathbf{\tau }}^{\mathrm{T}}}\right)  =  - \left( {\mathbf{f},\operatorname{div}\mathbf{\tau }}\right) \tag{3.4}\]

for all \( \left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0} \) .

\[\mathcal{J}\left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma };\mathbf{0}}\right)  \geq  C\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{1}^{2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{0,\mathbf{{div}}}^{2}}\right) ,\;\forall \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0}. \tag{3.5}\]

## 4. Finite element approximation

### 4.1. Subspaces and interpolation properties

Let \( \Omega \) be a polygon or a polyhedron. Let \( {\mathcal{T}}_{h} \) be a regular triangulation of \( \Omega \) into triangles or rectangles (tetrahedrons or rectangular parallelepiped in \( {\Re }^{3} \) ),cf. [17,9]. We introduce

\[{V}_{h} = \left\{  {v \in  {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) ;{v}_{{\left. \right| }_{K}} \in  {\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left( K\right) ,\forall K \in  {\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\right\}  , \tag{4.1}\]

where \( {\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left( K\right)  = {\mathcal{P}}_{1}\left( K\right) \) or \( = {\mathcal{Q}}_{1}\left( K\right) ,{\mathcal{P}}_{1}\left( K\right) \) is the space of linear polynomials and \( {\mathcal{Q}}_{1}\left( K\right) \) is the space of iso-parametric bilinear polynomials.

\[{W}_{h} = \left\{  {\mathbf{q} \in  H\left( {\operatorname{div};\Omega }\right) ;{\mathbf{q}}_{{\left. \right| }_{K}} \in  \mathcal{S}\left( K\right) ,\forall K \in  {\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\right\}  , \tag{4.2}\]

where \( \mathcal{S}\left( K\right)  = {RT}{N}_{0}\left( K\right) \) for triangles or tetrahedrons,or \( \mathcal{S}\left( K\right)  = {RT}{N}_{\left\lbrack  0\right\rbrack  }\left( K\right) \) for rectangles or rectangular parallelepiped, \( {RT}{N}_{0}\left( K\right) \) and \( {RT}{N}_{\left\lbrack  0\right\rbrack  }\left( K\right) \) are the lowest-order Raviart-Thomas-Nédélec flux elements,cf. [13].

Let \( {\mathcal{I}}_{h}u \in  {V}_{h} \) and \( {\Pi }_{h}\mathbf{p} \in  {W}_{h} \) be the interpolants to \( u \in  {H}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \) and \( \mathbf{p} \in  {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \) with \( \operatorname{div}\mathbf{p} \in  {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \) respectively. We have (cf. [17,9,13])

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{I}_{h}u - u\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + h{\begin{Vmatrix}{I}_{h}u - u\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} \leq  C{h}^{2}\parallel u{\parallel }_{2}, \tag{4.3}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\Pi }_{h}\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  {Ch}\parallel \mathbf{p}{\parallel }_{1}, \tag{4.4}\]

\[\left( {\operatorname{div}\left( {{\Pi }_{h}\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}}\right) ,\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{q}}_{h}}\right)  = 0\;\forall {\mathbf{q}}_{h} \in  {W}_{h}, \tag{4.5}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}\left( {\Pi }_{h}\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  {Ch}\parallel \operatorname{div}\mathbf{p}{\parallel }_{1}. \tag{4.6}\]

Remark 4.1. When \( {\mathcal{T}}_{h} \) is composed of quadrilaterals in \( {\Re }^{2} \) ,we can adopt the new variant constructed by [19,22] of the lowest-order Raviart-Thomas rectangular flux element, i.e.,

\[\mathcal{L}\left( K\right)  \mathrel{\text{ := }} \operatorname{span}\left\{  {{\left( 1,0\right) }^{\mathrm{T}},{\left( 0,1\right) }^{\mathrm{T}},{\left( x,y\right) }^{\mathrm{T}},\nabla  \times  \left( {{N}_{1} \circ  {F}_{K}^{-1}}\right) }\right\}  , \tag{4.7}\]

where \( {N}_{1} = \left( {1 + \xi }\right) \left( {1 + \eta }\right) /4 \) is the bilinear polynomial on the reference square \( \widehat{K} = \left\lbrack  {-1,1}\right\rbrack   \times  \left\lbrack  {-1,1}\right\rbrack \) and \( {F}_{K} : \widehat{K} \rightarrow  K \) is the usual invertible mapping,cf. [17,9]. For (4.7),there still hold (4.4)-(4.6).

In the case of deformed rectangular elements in \( {\mathfrak{R}}^{d} \) ,we may adopt variants constructed by [23] of Raviart-Thomas-Nédélec rectangular flux elements. However, in this case, (4.5) and (4.6) are generally no longer true. Instead, we only have

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}\left( {\Pi }_{h}\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  {Ch}\parallel \mathbf{p}{\parallel }_{2}. \tag{4.8}\]

(4.6) is more useful than (4.8),one reason is that for most problems we cannot bound \( \parallel \mathbf{p}{\parallel }_{2} \) by the \( {L}^{2} \) - norm of the right-hand side function in the analysis for the \( {L}^{2} \) -error bound,see Section 5 and see also [19] for other examples.

## 5. \( {L}^{2} \) -error bounds

In this section,using the Aubin-Nitsche duality argument,we obtain the \( {L}^{2} \) -error bound for the displacement.

#### 5.1.The Aubin-Nitsche duality argument

We first recall the classical Aubin-Nitsche duality argument (cf. [17,9]) and state it in a slightly different form, see also [19].

Let \( X \) be a Hilbert space,and let \( f\left( \cdot \right) \) be a bounded linear functional over \( X \) ,and let \( a\left( {\cdot , \cdot  }\right) \) be a bounded non-negative symmetric bilinear form over \( X \times  X \) . We consider a variational problem: Find \( u \in  X \) such that

\[a\left( {u,v}\right)  = f\left( v\right) ,\;\forall v \in  X. \tag{5.1}\]

A corresponding finite element problem is to find \( {u}_{h} \in  {X}_{h} \subset  X \) such that

\[a\left( {{u}_{h},v}\right)  = f\left( v\right) ,\;\forall v \in  {X}_{h}. \tag{5.2}\]

Here we always assume that (5.1) and (5.2) have solutions for any given \( f \in  {X}^{\prime }\left( {X}^{\prime }\right. \) denotes the dual of \( \left. X\right) \) .

The Aubin-Nitsche duality argument is as follows. Let \( Y \supset  X \) be another Hilbert space and \( g\left( \cdot \right) \) be any bounded linear functional over \( Y \) . We consider the following auxiliary variational problem: Find \( w \in  X \) such that

\[a\left( {w,v}\right)  = g\left( v\right) ,\;\forall v \in  X. \tag{5.3}\]

Then,let \( \widetilde{u},\widetilde{w} \in  {X}_{h} \) be any two functions. We have

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}u - {u}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{Y}^{2} \leq  a{\left( u - \widetilde{u},u - \widetilde{u}\right) }^{1/2}a{\left( w - \widetilde{w},w - \widetilde{w}\right) }^{1/2}. \tag{5.4}\]

Remark 5.1. Note that from Hahn-Banach theorem,we can take \( g \in  {Y}^{\prime } \) such that

\[g\left( {u - {u}_{h}}\right)  = {\begin{Vmatrix}u - {u}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{Y}^{2},\;\parallel g{\parallel }_{{Y}^{\prime }} = {\begin{Vmatrix}u - {u}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{Y}. \tag{5.5}\]

### 5.2. Regularity assumptions

Hypothesis (H1)-Given \( {\chi }_{0} \in  {\left( {L}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \) and \( {\kappa }_{0} \in  {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d \times  d} \) . Let \( z \in  U \) be the unique solution to the variational problem

\[{2\mu }\left( {\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{z}\right) }\right) ,\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) ) + \lambda \left( {\operatorname{div}\mathbf{z},\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  = \left( {{\mathbf{\chi }}_{0},\mathbf{v}}\right)  + {\int }_{{\Gamma }_{N}}{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\mathbf{\kappa }}_{0}\mathbf{n},\;\forall \mathbf{v} \in  U, \tag{5.6}\]

we assume that the following regularity holds:

\[\parallel \mathbf{z}{\parallel }_{2} + \lambda \parallel \operatorname{div}\mathbf{z}{\parallel }_{1} \leq  C\left\{  {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}_{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\kappa }}_{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1}}\right\}  . \tag{5.7}\]

Hypothesis (H2)-Given any \( {\mathbf{v}}_{0} \in  {\left( {H}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \cap  U \) . We assume that there exists \( {\mathbf{w}}_{0} \in  {\left( {H}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \cap  U \) such that

\[\operatorname{div}\mathbf{w} = \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{v}}_{0} \tag{5.8}\]

and

\[\parallel \mathbf{w}{\parallel }_{2} \leq  C{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1}. \tag{5.9}\]

Remark 5.2. For a convex polygon \( \Omega \) with \( {\Gamma }_{N} = \varnothing \) or \( {\Gamma }_{D} = \varnothing \) ,(H1) and (H2) hold,see [1,9] (see also [5,6] for a smooth domain). General statements seem not available in the literature.

The following proposition results from (H1) and (H2).

Proposition 5.1. Given \( {\chi }_{0} \in  {\left( {L}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d},{\mathbf{\kappa }}_{0} \in  {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d \times  d} \) and \( {\mathbf{v}}_{0} \in  {\left( {H}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \cap  U \) . Let \( \left( {\mathbf{u},p}\right)  \in  U \times  Q \) be the unique solution to the variational problem

\[{2\mu }\left( {\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{u}\right) }\right) ,\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) ) + \left( {p,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  = \left( {{\mathbf{\chi }}_{0},\mathbf{v}}\right)  + {\int }_{{\Gamma }_{N}}{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\mathbf{\kappa }}_{0}\mathbf{n},\;\forall \mathbf{v} \in  U, \tag{5.10}\]

\[\left( {\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u},q}\right)  - \frac{1}{\lambda }\left( {p,q}\right)  = \left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{v}}_{0},q}\right) ,\;\forall q \in  Q.\]

Assuming (H1) and (H2), we have

\[\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{2} + \parallel p{\parallel }_{1} \leq  C\left\{  {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}_{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\kappa }}_{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1}}\right\}  . \tag{5.11}\]

### 5.3. \( {L}^{2} \) -error bound

Now,we can derive the \( {L}^{2} \) -error bound for the displacement.

We consider an auxiliary variational problem: Find \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0} \) such that

\[\mathcal{L}\left( {\left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) ;\left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau }}\right) }\right)  = \left( {\mathbf{\chi },\mathbf{v}}\right) ,\;\forall \left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0}, \tag{5.12}\]

where \( \chi  \in  {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \) .

Theorem 5.1. (5.12) has a unique solution \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) \) . Assuming (H1) and (H2),we have

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{2} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{2} + \lambda {\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} \leq  C\parallel \mathbf{\chi }{\parallel }_{0}. \tag{5.13}\]

Proof. Problem (5.12) has a unique solution \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) \) ,because of Theorem 3.1. As usual,from (5.12) we can obtain a boundary-value problem in the distributional sense, which implies a necessary regularity under Hypotheses (H1) and (H2).

In (5.12),take \( \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0} \) and \( \tau  = \mathbf{0} \) to get

\[{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D} - {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\right)  = \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) }\right) \operatorname{Id} + \nabla \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }, \tag{5.14}\]

\[\frac{{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D} + {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*{D}^{\mathrm{T}}}}{2} - {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\right)  = {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) , \tag{5.15}\]

\[\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right)  =  - \frac{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{d}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{{div}}}^{ * }, \tag{5.16}\]

\[{2\mu }\left( {\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) ,\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  + \lambda \left( {\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  =  - \left( {\mathbf{\chi },\mathbf{v}}\right) , \tag{5.17}\]

where \( \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } \in  U \) ,div \( \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } \in  Q \) .

Applying (H1) to (5.17) to get

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{2} + \lambda {\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} \leq  C\parallel \mathbf{\chi }{\parallel }_{0}. \tag{5.18}\]

Note that

\[\left( {\frac{{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D} + {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*{D}^{\mathrm{T}}}}{2}, - {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  =  - {2\mu }\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D},{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)\]

\[=  - {2\mu }\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  + \frac{2\mu }{d}\left( {\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) ,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)\]

\[=  - {2\mu }\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{\nabla }\mathbf{v}}\right)  + \frac{2\mu }{d}\left( {\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) ,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  - {2\mu }\frac{1}{2}\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*\mathrm{\;T}} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{\nabla }\mathbf{v}}\right) \tag{5.19}\]

\[=  - {2\mu }\left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{v}}\right)  + \frac{2\mu }{d}\left( {\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) ,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)\]

\[=  - {2\mu }\frac{1}{2}\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*\mathrm{\;T}} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\nabla \mathbf{v}}\right)\]

and that, in view of (5.14),

\[\frac{1}{2}\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*\mathrm{\;T}} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{\nabla }\mathbf{v}}\right)  = \frac{1}{2}\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*{D}^{\mathrm{T}}} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D},\mathbf{\nabla }\mathbf{v}}\right)\]

\[= \frac{1}{2}\left( {{\left( \mathbf{\nabla }\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) }^{\mathrm{T}} - \mathbf{\nabla }\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{\nabla }\mathbf{v}}\right)\]

\[= \left( {\mathbf{\nabla }\mathbf{\operatorname{div}}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  - \left( {\mathbf{\nabla }\mathbf{\operatorname{div}}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{\nabla }\mathbf{v}}\right) \tag{5.20}\]

\[= \left( {\frac{\nabla \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } + {\left( \nabla \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) }^{\mathrm{T}}}{2},\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  - \left( {\nabla \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\nabla \mathbf{v}}\right)\]

\[= \left( {\varepsilon \left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) ,\varepsilon \left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  - \left( {\nabla \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\nabla \mathbf{v}}\right) ,\]

using (5.14)-(5.20) and taking \( \tau  = \mathbf{0} \) in (5.12),by a simple but tedious calculation we can have

\[\left\{  \begin{array}{l} {2\mu }\left( {\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\right) ,\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  + \frac{\lambda }{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\left( {\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) ,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  = \left( {-\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{v}}\right) , \\   - \frac{d\left( {{2\mu \lambda } - 1}\right)  + 4{\mu }^{2}}{{d}^{2}}\left( {\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  - \left( {\mathbf{V}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{V}\mathbf{v}}\right) ,\;\forall \mathbf{v} \in  U, \\  \left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },q}\right)  - \frac{1}{\lambda }\frac{\lambda }{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\left( {\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) ,q}\right)  = \left( {-\frac{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{d}\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },q}\right) ,\;\forall q \in  Q. \end{array}\right. \tag{5.21}\]

Applying Proposition 5.1 to (5.21), in view of (5.18) we have

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{2} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{1} \leq  C\parallel \mathbf{\chi }{\parallel }_{0}. \tag{5.22}\]

Therefore,(5.18) and (5.22) lead to (5.13),where \( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D} = {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\right)  + {\left( \mathbf{\nabla }\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) }^{D} \) and \( {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} \leq  C\parallel \mathbf{\chi }{\parallel }_{0} \) .

Corollary 5.1. Assuming the same hypotheses as in Theorem 5.1. Let \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) \) and \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}}\right) \) be the exact and approximate solutions,respectively. If \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right)  \in  {\left( {H}^{2}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \times  {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d \times  d} \) ,with \( \operatorname{div}\mathbf{\sigma } \in  {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \) ,then

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{2}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{1} + \parallel \mathbf{\operatorname{div}}\mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{1}}\right\}  . \tag{5.23}\]

Proof. In (5.12),we take \( \mathbf{\chi } = \mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h} \) . Let \( \left( {\widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}}\right) ,\left( {\widetilde{{\mathbf{u}}^{ * }},\widetilde{{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}}\right)  \in  {U}_{h} \times  {\sum }_{0,h} \) denote the interpolants to \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) \) and \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) \) ,respectively. Applying the Aubin-Nitsche duality argument described in Section 5.1,we have

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0}^{2} \leq  \mathcal{L}{\left( \left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - \widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{\sigma } - \widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) ;\left( \mathbf{u} - \widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{\sigma } - \widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) \right) }^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{L}{\left( \left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - \widetilde{{\mathbf{u}}^{ * }},\mathbf{\sigma } - \widetilde{{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) ;\left( \mathbf{u} - \widetilde{{\mathbf{u}}^{ * }},\mathbf{\sigma } - \widetilde{{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) \right) }^{\frac{1}{2}}\]

\[\leq  C{h}^{2}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{1}\parallel \operatorname{div}\mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{1}}\right\}  \left\{  {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{2} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{1}{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{1}}\right\}\]

\[\leq  C{h}^{2}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{1}\parallel \operatorname{div}\mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{1}}\right\}  \parallel \mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}{\parallel }_{0}, \tag{5.24}\]

which leads to the conclusion.

Remark 5.3. For symmetric stress we may use the linear \( {C}^{0} \) element for both displacement and stress (see Remarks 3.1 and 4.2). Assuming that the solutions associated with Hypotheses (H1) and (H2) are more regular (cf. (H3) in Section 6), we can obtain

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{3/2}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{2}}\right\}  , \tag{5.25}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{-1} \leq  C{h}^{2}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{2}}\right\}  . \tag{5.26}\]

## 6. Unequal-order \( {C}^{0} \) approximations

### 6.1. Basic results

Let \( h \) be the mesh-size of the triangulation of \( \Omega \) . We define

\[{\mathcal{I}}_{h}\left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma };\mathbf{f}}\right)  = {h}^{-2}{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{u}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}^{2} + {h}^{-2}{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u} - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma }\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}^{2} + \parallel \mathbf{{div}}\mathbf{\sigma } + \mathbf{f}{\parallel }_{0}^{2}. \tag{6.1}\]

A corresponding weak problem is to find \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0}^{S} \) such that

\[{\mathcal{L}}_{h}\left( {\left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) ;\left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau }}\right) }\right)  \mathrel{\text{ := }} {h}^{-2}\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{u}\right) ,{\mathbf{\tau }}^{D} - {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)\]

\[+ {h}^{-2}\left( {\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u} - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma }\right) \operatorname{div}\mathbf{v} - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\tau }\right) }\right)\]

\[+ \left( {\operatorname{div}\mathbf{\sigma },\operatorname{div}\mathbf{\tau }}\right)  =  - \left( {\mathbf{f},\operatorname{div}\mathbf{\tau }}\right) \tag{6.2}\]

for all \( \left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0}^{S} \) .

Theorem 6.1. There exists a constant \( C > 0 \) ,independent of \( \lambda \) and \( h \) ,such that

\[{\mathcal{I}}_{h}\left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma };\mathbf{0}}\right)  \geq  C\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{1}^{2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{0,\operatorname{div}}^{2}}\right)  + {h}^{-2}{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{u}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}^{2} + {h}^{-2}{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}u - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma }\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}^{2} \tag{6.3}\]

for all \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0}^{S} \) .

Proof. (6.3) is a direct consequence of (3.5),because of \( h \leq  C \) and \( {\sum }_{0}^{S} \subset  {\sum }_{0} \) .

Remark 6.1. Due to the factor \( {h}^{-2} \) ,we usually employ quadratic or cubic element for the displacement and linear or quadratic element for the symmetric stress. Note that only for \( {C}^{0} \) elements of order \( \leq  3 \) there occurs a locking phenomenon as \( \lambda  \rightarrow  \infty \) in the displacement-based method,cf. [3].

We introduce

\[{V}_{h}^{r} = \left\{  {v \in  {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) ;\left. {v}_{{ \mid  }_{K}} \in  {\mathcal{R}}_{r}\left( K\right) ,\forall K \in  {\mathcal{T}}_{h}\right| }\right\} \tag{6.4}\]

where \( {\mathcal{R}}_{r}\left( K\right)  = {\mathcal{P}}_{r}\left( K\right) \) or \( = {\mathcal{Q}}_{r}\left( K\right) \) ,with \( r \geq  1,{\mathcal{P}}_{r}\left( K\right) \) is the space of polynomials of the total order not greater than \( r \) and \( {\mathcal{Q}}_{r}\left( K\right) \) is the isoparametric space of polynomials of the order in each variable not greater than \( r \) .

Let \( {I}_{h}u \in  {V}_{h}^{r} \) be the interpolant to \( u \in  {H}^{r + 1}\left( \Omega \right) \) . We have the following interpolation property

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{I}_{h}u - u\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + h{\begin{Vmatrix}{I}_{h}u - u\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} \leq  C{h}^{r + 1}\parallel u{\parallel }_{r + 1}. \tag{6.5}\]

Define

\[{U}_{h} = {\left( {V}_{h}^{r + 1}\right) }^{d} \cap  U,\;{\sum }_{0,h}^{S} = {\left( {V}_{h}^{r}\right) }^{d \times  d} \cap  {\sum }_{0}^{S}. \tag{6.6}\]

The finite element problem of (6.2) is to find \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}}\right)  \in  {U}_{h} \times  {\sum }_{0,h}^{S} \) such that

\[{\mathcal{L}}_{h}\left( {\left( {{\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}}\right) ;\left( {{\mathbf{v}}_{h},{\mathbf{\tau }}_{h}}\right) }\right)  =  - \left( {\mathbf{f},\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\tau }}_{h}}\right) ,\forall \left( {{\mathbf{v}}_{h},{\mathbf{\tau }}_{h}}\right)  \in  {U}_{h} \times  {\sum }_{0,h}^{S}. \tag{6.7}\]

The following theorem easily follows from the standard analysis depicted in [9,17], because of coerciveness, consistency and (6.5).

Theorem 6.2. Let \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) \) and \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}}\right) \) be the exact and approximate solutions to (2.1) and (6.7),respectively. If \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right)  \in  {\left( {H}^{r + 2}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d \times  d} \times  {\left( {H}^{r + 1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d \times  d} \) ,then

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0,\mathbf{{div}}} \leq  C{h}^{r}\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{r + 1}}\right) , \tag{6.8}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}^{D}\right)  - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}\left( \mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\right)  - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{r + 1}\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{r + 1}}\right) . \tag{6.9}\]

Remark 6.2. Obviously, (6.8) is not optimal. Nonetheless, using (6.9) and the Aubin-Nitsche duality argument,we can recover the optimal-order \( {h}^{r + 1} \) for (6.8).

### 6.2. Recovery of the optimal-order \( {h}^{r + 1} \)

Define a \( d \times  d \) matrix \( M \) as follows:

\[{M\tau } = {\tau }^{D} + \frac{2\mu }{d\left( {{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\right) }\operatorname{tr}\left( \tau \right) \operatorname{Id}.\]

Clearly,

\[{M}^{2}\tau  = {\tau }^{D}\frac{4{\mu }^{2}}{d{\left( 2\mu  + d\lambda \right) }^{2}}\operatorname{tr}\left( \tau \right) \mathrm{{Id}}\]

\[\left( {{M}^{2}\tau ,\tau }\right)  = {\begin{Vmatrix}{\tau }^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0}^{2} + \frac{4{\mu }^{2}}{d{\left( 2\mu  + d\lambda \right) }^{2}}\parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \tau \right) {\parallel }_{0}^{2}.\]

We consider an auxiliary problem: Find \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0}^{S} \) such that

\[{\mathcal{L}}_{h}\left( {\left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) ;\left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau }}\right) }\right)  = \left( {{M}^{2}\mathbf{\chi },\mathbf{\tau }}\right) ,\;\forall \left( {\mathbf{v},\mathbf{\tau }}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0}^{S}, \tag{6.10}\]

where \( \chi  \in  {\sum }_{0}^{S} \) .

Before investigating (6.10), we give a regularity assumption as follows:

Hypothesis (H3)-Given \( {\chi }_{0} \in  {\left( {H}^{1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \) . Let \( z \in  U \) be the unique solution to the variational problem

\[{2\mu }\left( {\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{z}\right) ,\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  + \lambda \left( {\operatorname{div}\mathbf{z},\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  = \left( {{\mathbf{\chi }}_{0},\mathbf{v}}\right) ,\;\forall \mathbf{v} \in  U, \tag{6.11}\]

we assume that the following regularity holds:

\[\parallel \mathbf{z}{\parallel }_{3} + \lambda \parallel \operatorname{div}\mathbf{z}{\parallel }_{2} \leq  C{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}_{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1}. \tag{6.12}\]

Note that for a smooth domain with \( {\Gamma }_{D} = \varnothing \) or \( {\Gamma }_{N} = \varnothing \) (6.12) holds,see [5,6].

Theorem 6.3. Assuming (H3). Problem (6.10) has a unique solution \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) \) ,satisfying

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) {\parallel }_{0}}\right) . \tag{6.13}\]

Moreover,there exists \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{0},{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}}\right)  \in  U \times  {\sum }_{0}^{S} \) such that

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{u}}^{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{3} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{2} \leq  C\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) {\parallel }_{0}}\right) , \tag{6.14}\]

\[\operatorname{div}\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right)  \equiv  \mathbf{0}, \tag{6.15}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D} - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\right)  - \left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{{0}^{D}} - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{0}\right) \right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{2}\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) {\parallel }_{0}}\right) , \tag{6.16}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right)  - \left( \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{0} - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}\right) \right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{2}\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) {\parallel }_{0}}\right) . \tag{6.17}\]

Proof. Problem (6.10) has a unique solution \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) \) ,because of Theorem 6.1

Similar to Theorem 5.1,take \( \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0} \) and \( \mathbf{\tau } = \mathbf{0} \) in (6.10) to get a boundary-value problem in the distributional sense as follows:

\[\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right)  =  - {h}^{2}\frac{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{d}\left( {\operatorname{div}\mathbf{{div}}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } + \frac{4{\mu }^{2}}{{\left( 2\mu  + d\lambda \right) }^{2}}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) }\right) , \tag{6.18}\]

\[{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D} - {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\right)  = {h}^{2}\left( {{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right)  + {\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}}\right) , \tag{6.19}\]

\[{2\mu }\left( {\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right) ,\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  + \lambda \left( {\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  =  - {2\mu }\left( {{\mathbf{\chi }}^{D},\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  - \frac{4{\mu }^{2}}{d\left( {{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\right) }\left( {\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) ,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right) , \tag{6.20}\]

where \( \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } \in  U \) , \( \operatorname{div}\operatorname{\mathbf{d} \mathbf{i} \mathbf{v} }{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } \in  Q \) .

\[\left\{  \begin{array}{l} {2\mu }\left( {\mathbf{s}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\right) ,\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  + \frac{\lambda }{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\left( {\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) ,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  = \left( {-\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{v}}\right) \\   + {h}^{2}\frac{\left( {d - 4{\mu }^{2}}\right) \left( {{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\right) }{{2\mu }{d}^{2}}\left( {\operatorname{div}d{\mathbf{v}}^{ * } + \frac{4{\mu }^{2}}{{\left( 2\mu  + d\lambda \right) }^{2}}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) ,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right) \\  \forall \mathbf{v} \in  U, \\  \left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * },q}\right)  - \frac{1}{\lambda }\frac{\lambda }{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\left( {\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) ,q}\right) \\   =  - {h}^{2}\frac{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{{d}^{2}}\left( {\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } + \frac{4{\mu }^{2}}{2{\mu }^{2} + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) ,q}\right) ,\forall q \in  Q. \end{array}\right. \tag{6.21}\]

It can be easily seen that

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + \lambda {\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\chi }^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) {\parallel }_{0}}\right) , \tag{6.22}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) {\parallel }_{0}}\right) . \tag{6.23}\]

We now consider another variational problem: Find \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}^{0},{p}^{0}}\right)  \in  U \times  Q \) such that

\[\left\{  \begin{array}{l} {2\mu }\left( {\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{0}\right) ,\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( \mathbf{v}\right) }\right)  + \left( {{p}^{0},\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}}\right)  =  - \left( {-\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * },\mathbf{v}}\right) , \\  \left( {\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{0},q}\right)  - \frac{1}{\lambda }\left( {{p}^{0},q}\right)  = 0. \end{array}\right. \tag{6.24}\]

Applying (H3) to (6.24), in view of (6.22) we have

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{u}}^{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{3} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{p}^{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{2} \leq  C{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} \leq  \left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) {\parallel }_{0}}\right) . \tag{6.25}\]

Define

\[{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0} = {2\mu }\mathbf{\varepsilon }\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{0}\right)  + {p}^{0}\operatorname{Id} \in  {\sum }_{0}^{S}, \tag{6.26}\]

we have

\[\operatorname{div}\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }}\right)  \equiv  \mathbf{0},\;{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{2} \leq  \left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) {\parallel }_{0}}\right) , \tag{6.27}\]

\[{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0D} = {2\mu }{\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{0}\right) ,\;\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}\right)  = \left( {{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\right) \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{0}, \tag{6.28}\]

\[{p}^{0} = \lambda \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{0} = \frac{\lambda }{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}\right) , \tag{6.29}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D} - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\right)  - \left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0D} - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{0}\right) \right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} = {\begin{Vmatrix}{h}^{2}\left( {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( \mathbf{{div}}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right)  + {\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{2}\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\chi }}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}}\right) ,\]

(6.30)

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right)  + \left( \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{0} - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}\right) \right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}\]

\[= {\begin{Vmatrix}-{h}^{2}\frac{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{d}\left( \operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } + \frac{4{\mu }^{2}}{{\left( 2\mu  + d\lambda \right) }^{2}}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) \right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{2}\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\chi }^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }} + \parallel \operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\chi }\right) {\parallel }_{0}}\right) .\]

(6.31)

Corollary 6.1. Assuming the same hypotheses as in Theorem 6.3. Let \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) \) and \( \left( {{\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}}\right) \) be the exact and approximate solutions,respectively. If \( \left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right)  \in  {\left( {H}^{r + 2}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d} \times  {\left( {H}^{r + 1}\left( \Omega \right) \right) }^{d \times  d} \) ,then

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{r + 1}\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{r + 1}}\right) . \tag{6.32}\]

Proof. In (6.10),taking \( \mathbf{\chi } = \mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h} \) . Let \( \left( {\widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}}\right)  \in  {U}_{h} \times  {\sum }_{0,h}^{S} \) and \( \left( {{\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{0},{\widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}}^{0}}\right)  \in  {U}_{h} \times  {\sum }_{0,h}^{S}\left( {\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) \) be the interpolants to \( \left( {\mathbf{u},{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}}\right) \) ,and respectively.

By the Aubin-Nitsche duality argument, we have

\[{M}^{2}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}}\right) \mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{n} \leq  {\mathcal{L}}_{h}{\left( \left( \mathbf{u} - \widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{\sigma } - \widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) ;\left( \mathbf{u} - \widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{\sigma } - \widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) \right) }^{1/2}\]

\[\times  {\mathcal{L}}_{h}{\left( \left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - {\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{0},{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } - {\widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}}^{0}\right) ;\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - {\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{0},{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } - {\widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}}^{0}\right) \right) }^{1/2}, \tag{6.33}\]

where, in view of (6.27), (6.30), (6.31), (6.25) and interpolation properties, we have

\[{\mathcal{L}}_{h}{\left( \left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - {\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{0},{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } - {\widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}}^{0}\right) ;\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - {\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{0},{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * } - {\widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}}^{0}\right) \right) }^{1/2}\]

\[\leq  {\mathcal{L}}_{h}{\left( \left( {\mathbf{u}}^{0} - {\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{0},{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0} - {\widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}}^{0}\right) ;\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{0} - {\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{0},{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0} - {\widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}}^{0}\right) \right) }^{1/2} + C{h}^{-1}{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{*D} - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( {\mathbf{u}}^{ * }\right)  - \left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0D} - 2\mu {\mathbf{\varepsilon }}^{D}\left( u\right) \right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}\]

\[+ C{h}^{-1}{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{ * } - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{ * }\right)  - \left( \operatorname{div}{\mathbf{u}}^{0} - \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}\right) \right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}\]

\[\leq  {Ch}\left\{  {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{u}}^{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{3} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{0}\end{Vmatrix}}_{2}}\right\}   + {Ch}\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}\operatorname{tr}\begin{Vmatrix}\left( {{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}^{D}}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}\right)\]

\[\leq  {Ch}\left( {{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}^{D}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0}}\right) , \tag{6.34}\]

\[{\mathcal{L}}_{h}{\left( \left( \mathbf{u} - \widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{\sigma } - \widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) ;\left( \mathbf{u} - \widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{\sigma } - \widetilde{\mathbf{\sigma }}\right) \right) }^{1/2} \leq  C{h}^{r}\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{r + 1}}\right) . \tag{6.35}\]

It follows that

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{tr}\left( {\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}^{D}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{r + 1}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{r + 1}}\right\}  , \tag{6.36}\]

which, together with (6.9), leads to the conclusion.

## 7. Conclusion

In this paper we have developed a least-squares method for the stress-displacement approximation of the linear elasticity problem. This new method is coercive and allows the use of any conforming element for all variables.

(1) If we use the \( {C}^{0} \) element of order \( r \geq  1 \) for the displacement and the flux element of order \( s \geq  0 \) for the (nonsymmetric) stress, then

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{\min \left( {r,s + 1}\right) }\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{s + 1}}\right) , \tag{7.1}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0,\mathbf{{div}}} \leq  C{h}^{\min \left( {r,s + 1}\right) }\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{s + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{{div}}\mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{s + 1}}\right) . \tag{7.2}\]

(2) If we use the equal-order \( {C}^{0} \) element of order \( r \geq  1 \) for both stress and displacement,then

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0,\mathbf{{div}}} \leq  C{h}^{r}\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{r + 1}}\right) . \tag{7.3}\]

(3) If we use the unequal-order \( {C}^{0} \) elements of order \( \left( {r + 1,r}\right) ,\left( {r \geq  1}\right) \) ,for displacement and stress,by means of a mesh-dependent method we then have

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}{\mathbf{\sigma }}^{D} - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}^{D}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} + \frac{1}{{2\mu } + {d\lambda }}{\begin{Vmatrix}\operatorname{tr}\left( \mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}\right) \end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{r + 1}\left( {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 2} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{r + 1}}\right) . \tag{7.4}\]

Assuming regularity assumptions (H1),(H2) and (H3). Note that for pure displacement \( \left( {{\Gamma }_{N} = \varnothing }\right) \) and pure traction \( \left( {{\Gamma }_{D} = \varnothing }\right) \) problems,in the case of a convex polygonal domain,(H1) and (H2) hold,while in the case of a smooth domain, all these assumptions hold.

Corresponding to (1) and (2), \( {L}^{2} \) and \( {H}^{-1} \) -error bounds are as follows:

\( \left( {1}^{\prime }\right) \)

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{\min \left( {r,s + 1}\right)  + 1}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{s + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{\operatorname{div}}\mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{s + 1}}\right\}  , \tag{7.5}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{-1} \leq  C{h}^{\min \left( {r,s + 1}\right)  + 2}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{s + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{\operatorname{div}}\mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{s + 1}}\right\}  , \tag{7.6}\]

where for (7.5) \( r \geq  1,s \geq  0 \) and for (7.6) \( r \geq  2,s \geq  1 \) .

\( \left( {2}^{\prime }\right) \)

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{r + \frac{1}{2}}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{r + 1}}\right\}  , \tag{7.7}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{-1} \leq  C{h}^{r + 1}\left\{  {\parallel \mathbf{u}{\parallel }_{r + 1} + \parallel \mathbf{\sigma }{\parallel }_{r + 1}}\right\}  . \tag{7.8}\]

In particular, in the case of a convex polygonal domain, for pure displacement and pure traction problems we use the (bi)linear \( {C}^{0} \) for displacement and the lowest-order flux element for (nonsymmetric) stress and we obtain

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{1} + {\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{\sigma } - {\mathbf{\sigma }}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  {Ch}\parallel \mathbf{f}{\parallel }_{0}, \tag{7.9}\]

\[{\begin{Vmatrix}\mathbf{u} - {\mathbf{u}}_{h}\end{Vmatrix}}_{0} \leq  C{h}^{2}\parallel \mathbf{f}{\parallel }_{1}. \tag{7.10}\]

For general boundary conditions \( \left( {{\Gamma }_{D} \neq  \varnothing \text{ and }{\Gamma }_{N} \neq  \varnothing }\right) \) ,so long as (H1) and (H2) hold,(7.9) and (7.10) are true in the case of a convex polygonal domain.

After submitting this paper, one referee draws our attention to a recent work [27] by Cai and Starke. These two papers both take stress and displacement as simultaneous variables, but we point out that our scheme and our analysis are different from theirs. Their error estimates (4-8) are the same as ours (7.2),but we get optimal error bounds (7.1). Moreover,we have established the \( {L}^{2} \) -error bounds (7.5) for the displacement. We in addition consider equal/unequal-order continuous approximations for all variables and get the corresponding error bounds (7.3), (7.4) and (7.7).
